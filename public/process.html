How Our Financial Recovery Services Work

When a property is sold through foreclosure, tax sale, or a similar public process, the final sale price may exceed the total amount owed. The remaining balance — after all liens, taxes, fees, and obligations are satisfied — is known as surplus or overage funds. These funds are held through county procedures and may be available to former owners or related parties.

Our team reviews publicly available records to identify whether surplus funds are associated with a property you previously owned. Once identified, your dedicated recovery specialist will walk you through the claims process from start to finish, handling the complexity so you don't have to.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

We understand that surplus fund recovery is an unfamiliar process for most people. Below are the most common questions our clients ask before starting their claim — answered plainly, without legal jargon.

01

What are surplus funds?

Surplus funds — sometimes called overage funds — are the remaining balance left over after a property is sold through a foreclosure, tax sale, or public auction and the total amount owed (taxes, liens, fees, and obligations) is satisfied. If a property sells for more than what was owed, the difference may be held through county procedures and could be available to the former owner.

02

Who may qualify to receive surplus funds?

Former property owners, heirs, and certain related parties may be eligible to claim surplus funds associated with a property that was sold through a foreclosure or tax sale. Eligibility is determined by public records and county-specific procedures. Our team reviews available records to assess whether a claim may be viable for your specific situation.

03

How does the recovery process work?

We begin with a confidential records review to identify whether surplus funds may be associated with your property. If a potential claim is found, your dedicated recovery specialist guides you through each step — from document preparation and filing to coordinating with the appropriate county office — until the funds are successfully recovered on your behalf.

04

How long does the process typically take?

Timelines vary depending on the county, the complexity of the claim, and the responsiveness of the relevant agencies. Most claims are resolved within 60 to 180 days from the point of engagement. Your recovery specialist will provide a realistic timeline estimate once your case is reviewed and we have a clearer picture of the jurisdiction involved.

05

Are there any upfront costs or fees?

No. Piedmont Triad Equity Solutions operates entirely on a contingency basis — meaning we are only compensated if and when your claim is successfully recovered. There are no upfront fees, no out-of-pocket expenses, and no hidden charges at any stage of the process. Our financial recovery services carry zero financial risk to you.

06

Is the government holding my funds — can I claim them myself?

In many cases, surplus funds are held by a county clerk, court registry, or similar government office. While it is technically possible to file a claim independently, the process involves specific legal filings, documentation requirements, and deadlines that vary by jurisdiction. Many former owners are unaware these funds exist at all. We handle the entire process so you don't have to navigate it alone.

07

How is my personal information protected?

Confidentiality is a core commitment at every stage of our engagement. Your personal details, property information, and claim status are handled with strict discretion and are never shared with third parties beyond what is required to process your claim. We take the privacy of every client seriously, regardless of claim size or complexity.

08

Can you guarantee that funds will be recovered?

We cannot guarantee a specific outcome, as every claim is subject to public records, county procedures, and applicable legal requirements beyond our control. What we can guarantee is a thorough, professional review of your case and dedicated representation throughout the process. If we do not believe a viable claim exists, we will tell you clearly before proceeding.

09

Am I obligated to proceed after submitting my information?

Absolutely not. Submitting your information for an initial review carries no obligation to proceed. Our preliminary review is provided at no cost and with no commitment required on your part. You are free to decide whether to move forward after receiving our findings — there is no pressure at any stage of the process.

10

What happens after I submit my claim information?

Once you submit your details, a dedicated recovery specialist will be assigned to your case within one to two business days. They will conduct a confidential review of publicly available records, confirm whether a potential claim exists, and reach out to walk you through the next steps. You will have a single point of contact throughout the entire process.
